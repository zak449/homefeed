# Editorial seed brief — Gwaky's first 50 takes

This is the creative brief for the seed content that gives Gwaky its voice. The
brand promise is **"the comment section real estate never had."** Today the seed
takes read like measured analyst commentary. They should read like a friend who
just got back from the open house and has Things To Say.

This is the highest-leverage product move you can make this week. Polished UX
fails if the first 5 takes a new user reads sound like Realtor.com.

---

## The brief

Pay 5 friends $200 each to drop 10 takes apiece across local listings. Total
budget: $1,000. Time: 1-2 hours per writer. Output: 50 seed takes that
demonstrate the voice and pull new users into a vibe.

### Tone ladder

Each writer covers a mix of these tones so the feed has rhythm:

- **3 measured-analyst takes** — the Marcus L. flavor we already have. Numbers,
  comps, factual observations. These build credibility.
- **3 honest-neighbor takes** — first-person warnings, lived experience, things
  you only know if you live there. ("The trash truck wakes the whole block at
  4:47 AM.")
- **2 unhinged-with-receipts takes** — the takes people screenshot and share.
  Strong opinion, specific detail. ("This kitchen was renovated by someone
  who watched 8 hours of HGTV. Look at the cabinets. They open into the
  fridge. They tested NOTHING.")
- **1 contrarian agent-vs-neighbor take** — directly contradicts the listing
  description. ("Listing says 'quiet street.' I walked there at 3 PM Tuesday.
  There were two leaf blowers and a guy power-washing his Tesla.")
- **1 funny-but-true take** — humor with a real point. ("If this house could
  speak it would whisper 'help me.' But the price is fair.")

### Voice rules

- Always first person, never "users" or "buyers."
- Specific over general. "$15-20k" beats "expensive." "3-minute walk to the
  trail" beats "convenient location."
- Earned authority — say HOW you know what you know. ("Lived two doors down
  for 3 years.")
- Never use the word "buyer." Try "you" or "anyone moving in."
- Never use the words "amenity," "lifestyle," "showcases," "boasts,"
  "presents." These are agent words. We are not agents.
- It's okay to be wrong if you're honest about it. Hedge with "I think" or
  "could be wrong but" — uncertainty reads as real.
- Drop names: contractors, schools, restaurants, neighbors. Specificity is
  trust.

### Length

- Sweet spot: **2-4 sentences per take.** Not a paragraph essay.
- Twitter cadence. Punch first, evidence second.
- Hard ceiling: 60 words.

### Tags writers can use

When applicable, assign one of: `red-flag`, `intel`, `price-check`,
`personal-story`, `block-vibes`. (These map to the existing app filters.)

## 50 prompts your seeders can draw from

Send the 5 writers this list, ask each to claim 10 prompts, distribute the
tone-ladder across them. Each prompt is a starting point — they should
adapt to the actual listing they're writing about.

### Red flags (10)

1. "Walk by at [time of day] and you'll hear/see/smell [specific thing] that
   the photos hide."
2. "The previous owner did [unpermitted renovation] — ask for the city
   permit history."
3. "This block had [event/incident] in [year]. Look it up before you commit."
4. "The HOA here is run by [character description] and they will fine you
   for [specific petty thing]."
5. "There's a [drainage / mold / foundation] issue on this street. I've seen
   [number] neighbors deal with it."
6. "The realtor staged this house aggressively. [Specific furniture trick]
   is hiding [specific flaw]."
7. "This price is [N]% above what a comparable property sold for [time
   ago] on [nearby street]."
8. "Listing claims [feature]. I went and verified — that's not accurate
   because [why]."
9. "The school redistricting changed [year]. This address now feeds into
   [specific school], not the one most people assume."
10. "This property was listed [N] times before and pulled. Sellers are
    fishing. Walk."

### Intel (10)

11. "Best part of this location: [specific underrated thing] — most listings
    don't mention it."
12. "There's a [restaurant / coffee shop / trail / park] within [N min] walk
    that locals love and the listing won't tell you about."
13. "Foundation work was done in [year]. Look for the permit. The previous
    sellers were transparent about it."
14. "The neighbor at [direction] is a [character type] — you'll either love
    or hate them. Either way, you should know."
15. "The [feature] in the photos is real and it's better than they made it
    look. [Specific reason]."
16. "If you work from home: the [cell coverage / wifi / quietness] situation
    on this block is [actual report]."
17. "I lived 3 doors down. The thing nobody mentions: [specific lifestyle
    detail like 'morning fog' or 'how the sun hits the kitchen at 7am']."
18. "Builder of this home was [specific local name] in the [decade]. They
    were known for [pro / con]."
19. "Easy fix: the [obvious flaw the listing tries to hide] is actually [low
    cost / DIY] to address. Don't overweight it."
20. "Comparable sale data: in the last 6 months, [N] homes within [N
    blocks] sold for [range]. This list price is [interpretation]."

### Price check (10)

21. "At $[X]M you're paying a premium for [feature]. Worth it if [condition],
    not worth it if [other condition]."
22. "This is priced [%] above the block average. Justification: [reason] or
    sellers are testing."
23. "Per square foot, this is [N] vs. neighborhood average of [N]. That gap
    is [explanation]."
24. "Rental comp: similar units in this building have rented for $[range].
    The asking rent is [interpretation]."
25. "If you negotiate: I'd start at [number]. Here's why: [specific market
    signal]."
26. "[Feature] adds maybe $[amount] to the value, but the listing is asking
    $[more]. Math doesn't work."
27. "Property taxes for this address are public — they're $[range] / year.
    Factor that in."
28. "Insurance in this zip is [reason] — get a quote before you submit
    an offer."
29. "Comparable that just sold: [address] at $[X]. That's your floor."
30. "DOM is [N] days. In this market, that signals [reason]."

### Block vibes (10)

31. "Tuesday mornings here: [observation]. Friday evenings: [observation].
    Sundays: [observation]."
32. "The street parking situation is [reality]. The listing's 'plenty of
    street parking' line is [accurate / spin]."
33. "Sun direction: kitchen gets [time] light. Bedroom faces [direction].
    Backyard is [shade / sun] in the afternoon."
34. "Cell service inside the house: [carrier] is [signal]. [Carrier] is
    [signal]. Wifi from [ISP] is the only real option."
35. "Trash day is [day]. The trucks come at [time]. You will hear them."
36. "The HOA in this complex sends [N] emails a week and they're [tone]."
37. "Walking the dog at 6 AM here is [vibe]. Same walk at 6 PM is [other
    vibe]. The block has two personalities."
38. "Your neighbors at [position]: [archetype]. Your neighbors at [position]:
    [archetype]. This shapes daily life more than the kitchen."
39. "The light pollution at night is [level] — if you care about that for
    [reason], take note."
40. "Sound: [specific source] is audible from [room]. Live with it for a
    week and decide."

### Personal stories (10)

41. "I bought a house on this block in [year]. The thing I wish I'd known:
    [specific lesson]."
42. "I was the buyer that walked away from this listing last cycle. Here's
    what I saw at the inspection."
43. "I rented in this exact unit / building for [duration]. The honest pros:
    [list]. The honest cons: [list]."
44. "I sold a house 4 blocks from here. The mistake I made on price was
    [specific]. Don't repeat it."
45. "I drove by 3 times before submitting an offer last spring. Each time
    showed me something the photos didn't."
46. "I had this exact floor plan in [other city]. The thing that gets old:
    [specific design flaw]."
47. "My contractor said about a house like this: '[quoted advice].' Worth
    [N] hours of consult to verify."
48. "I almost bought in this neighborhood in [year]. The reason I didn't is
    still true today: [reason]."
49. "We renovated a house on this street last summer. Permitting through
    [city dept] takes [duration]. Budget that."
50. "My parents moved to this zip in [year]. Their take after [years]:
    [verdict]."

## How to brief the writers

Send each writer:

1. This document.
2. A spreadsheet with: 10 prompts (claimed), 10 listing URLs from gwaky.com
   they can write about, the tone-ladder expectation.
3. A deadline (48 hours is enough).
4. Payment terms ($200 on delivery via Venmo / Zelle).

Tell them: "We need 10 takes by Friday. Tone should range from 'thoughtful
neighbor' to 'unhinged friend warning you off a bad house.' Specificity wins.
Length: 2-4 sentences. No 'amenity' or 'lifestyle.' Write like you're texting
a friend who's about to make a bad decision."

After delivery, edit lightly for typos, light moderation, no PII (don't include
real neighbors' names). Drop them into the database via a one-off script:

```bash
# Spreadsheet → CSV → import script. The seed-takes.csv columns:
# listingId, authorName, authorLocation, role (neighbor|local|past_renter|
#   buyer|seller|drive_by|other), tag (red-flag|intel|price-check|
#   personal-story|block-vibes|null), content
npx tsx scripts/import-seed-takes.ts seed-takes.csv
```

If `scripts/import-seed-takes.ts` doesn't exist, I can write it in 15 min. Just
ask.
